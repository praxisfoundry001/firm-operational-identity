# The Firm as Operational Identity

## A Structural Relocation of the Theory of the Firm

This repository contains the source text for **The Firm as Operational Identity: A Structural Relocation of the Theory of the Firm**.

This paper proposes a structural relocation of the theory of the firm. It argues that classic theories of the firm are not simply wrong. Transaction-cost theory, nexus-of-contracts theory, property-rights theory, legal-entity theory, accounting-entity theory, capability theory, and hierarchy theory each identify a real surface of firm life.

The problem begins when any one surface is asked to become the whole object.

## Core claim

A firm is the continuing operational identity under which transactions, obligations, and labor engagements update the same answerability-bearing “it.” In more formal terms, the firm is a ladder-conditioned higher-order economic object: the cross-field-state-bearing operational identity under which exchange, credit, and labor are bounded in cross-propagating relation.

## Why this matters

This definition helps clarify hard cases in firm theory, including sole proprietors, shell entities, holding companies, franchise systems, platforms, contractor networks, joint ventures, and firms that survive deep restructuring.

The practical diagnostic is simple:

**Ask what updates the same operational “it.”**

## What this is not

This paper does not offer a new empirical model of firm behavior. It does not replace economic theory, legal analysis, accounting practice, organizational theory, or firm-specific empirical study. It offers a structural object that lets existing theories be placed more precisely.

## How it was done

The paper builds on the prior Structural Flow economics foundation, which identifies exchange, credit, labor, firm, integrated record, LIRI, and RH as the minimum structural foundation of economics.
